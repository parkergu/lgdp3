Abstract

Visual imitation learning has made significant progress in the field of robotic manipulation, but existing methods often fail in precision-critical tasks due to insufficient fine-grained geometric understanding. We propose LGDP3, a novel visuomotor policy that addresses this by integrating a dedicated local geometric feature extraction module into a 3D diffusion framework. Our perception module analyzes point cloud neighborhoods from a single depth camera to learn critical geometric details (e.g., edges, corners, and planes). Distinct from hierarchical architectures like PointNet++ that rely on aggressive downsampling, our non-hierarchical design explicitly preserves high-frequency spatial details often lost during set abstraction. By conditioning the decision-making process on this enriched geometric representation, our policy generates more precise and robust actions. In comprehensive evaluations across 8 simulation and 2 real-world tasks representative of industrial automation, LGDP3 consistently outperformed the strong baseline (DP3), achieving a 5.5% relative improvement in simulation and 23.5% in the real world. The policy also demonstrated significant generalization to variations in object position, appearance, and lighting conditions. These extensive evaluation results highlight the critical role of local geometric feature extraction in learning robust 3D representations for precise and practical robotic manipulation.

Method Overview

Overview of our policy (LGDP3)

Above: (a) The policy is trained end-to-end using expert demonstrations. (b) During evaluation, the trained policy performs closed-loop control using environmental observations. Below: The Perception module extracts a 3D Representation from an input point cloud using an encoder featuring Conv1D layers and our core Local Geometric Feature Extraction module. Simultaneously, it encodes the robot state into a Proprioception Representation via MLP. The Decision module, a diffusion-based backbone, conditions on these fused representations to generate the final action sequence through a denoising process.

Architecture of our Local Geometric Feature Extraction Module

We design a lightweight, non-hierarchical local geometric feature extraction module. Our ablation studies demonstrate that this design is more suitable for 3D-based Diffusion Policy than the standard PointNet++.

This diagram illustrates the data flow for an input of N=1024 points with C=256 features. The Group block uses K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) to find K=32 neighbors (a value determined by ablation), resulting in a (1024, 32, 256) tensor. The Pre Feature Extraction block then applies a shared module of Conv1D-ReLU layer. An Aggregate block performs Max Pooling to achieve permutation invariance and reduce the tensor to (1024, 256). Finally, the Post Feature Extraction block applies another Conv1D-ReLU layer to produce the final feature representation.
